Assignment 2

Dataset: Greenhouse Gas Emissions 1990-2018


White-Hat Write-Up

The dataset used is the Greenhouse Gas Emissions Dataset, which includes data on the CO2-equivalent greenhouse gas emissions per person by year for up to 94 countries (depending on the year) spanning from 2015 through 2024. Only data from 2015 through 2022 was used in the visualization, as 2023 and 2024 each had data for significantly fewer countries (46-48 as opposed to 84-94 for the earlier years). Including these final two years would have dramatically reduced the scope of the visualization, which could have exacerbated any missing data biases present in the dataset, as it is unknown whether roughly half of the countries from the earlier years are missing at random or not. For full transparency, the end date of 2022 is clearly and prominently communicated in both the title and tooltips of the visualization, which shows how countries' CO2-equivalent emissions per person have changed over time. Some of the countries present in the dataset did not have data available for either 2015 or 2022, so these countries were dropped so that the same time period is used for all countries visualized. (Tableau defaulted to setting such countries change in per-capita GHG emissions to zero, but since that could be misleading, implying that the change was truly zero when the actual change is unknown, the change values for these countries were set to null instead.) Since lower levels of greenhouse gas emissions are generally perceived to be a positive change, the color scale was chosen such that darker greens indicate larger drops in GHG emissions per person, while darker reds indicate larger increases. Importantly, a neutral light tan color appears not exactly in between the minimum and maximum values, but at zero, such that a neutral color corresponds with a neutral change. To further increase transparency and detail, hovering over each country on the main world map visualization opens a bar chart that shows how GHG emissions per person in that country evolved over the entire seven-year period, which adds an extra level of nuance compared to just the end minus start value, allowing users to easily explore country-level patterns throughout the relevant period. For these secondary bar chart visualizations, because GHG emissions is a quantitative, absolute value, the y-axis starts at zero so that the relative sizes of the bars do not mislead readers into thinking that the magnitudes of the changes are larger than their actual values. However, to allow for easier comparison of years within a country, the bars are given their own color scale. Finally, the tooltips communicate the exact values of the 2015 to 2022 change in greenhouse gas emissions per person to allow for more precise reading of the data compared to what the color legend might be able to depict on its own. To create the white-hat visualization, the dataset was first opened in a Google Sheets document to perform some basic exploration of the dataset (answering questions such as "What do the columns mean?" and "How many unique countries are there per year?"). Then, a Tableau workbook was created to design the primary visualization in one sheet and the secondary bar charts in another. Dealing with the fact that the dataset treated the year as an attribute as opposed to a dimension and creating the appropriate filters and calculated fields to address that concern took the most time. In total, it took one person roughly three hours to complete this visualization. AI tools were not used to create this visualization.
